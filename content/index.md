 Suggested Architectural Framework for an ESL Guide

To keep your reference modular and navigable, structure each grammar point around four consistent pillars:

1. **Form / Structure Matrix**: Formulas showing how the pattern is constructed (e.g., $Subject + have/has + Past\ Participle$).
2. **Concept & Timeline Visuals**: Explaining *when* and *why* to use it, paired with visual markers (e.g., past action with present relevance).
3. **Contrasting Pairs**: Directly contrasting easily confused structures (e.g., *Present Perfect* vs. *Past Simple*).
4. **Common Pitfalls & L1 Interference**: Highlights of frequent learner errors (e.g., misuse of articles, overusing continuous tenses with state verbs).

---
"Parts of speech" is standard, but in ESL materials, you'll often see it named **Word Classes** or **Building Blocks of English** to make it feel more functional than abstract.

A well-structured ESL library typically groups grammar into **Functional Domains** rather than just traditional categories. Below is a complete structural breakdown you can use to expand your blueprint.

## Comprehensive ESL Reference Architecture

**1. Word Classes (Parts of Speech & Lexical Items)**

* **Nouns**: Countable vs. uncountable, plural forms, proper nouns, abstract nouns.
* **Determiners & Articles**: Definite (*the*), indefinite (*a/an*), zero article, quantifiers (*some*, *many*, *few*).
* **Pronouns**: Subject, object, possessive, reflexive, relative (*who*, *which*, *that*), demonstrative (*this*, *that*).
* **Verbs**: Auxiliary (*be*, *do*, *have*), modal verbs (*can*, *should*, *must*), phrasal verbs, state vs. action verbs.
* **Adjectives**: Attributive vs. predicative, order of adjectives (OSASCOMP), comparative & superlative forms.
* **Adverbs**: Manner, frequency, time, place, degree; adverb placement in sentences.
* **Prepositions**: Time (*in*, *on*, *at*), place, movement/direction, dependent prepositions after verbs/adjectives.
* **Conjunctions & Connectors**: Coordinating (*FANBOYS*), subordinating (*because*, *although*), discourse markers (*however*, *furthermore*).

**2. The Tense & Aspect System**

* **Present Tenses**: Present Simple, Present Continuous, Present Perfect, Present Perfect Continuous.
* **Past Tenses**: Past Simple, Past Continuous, Past Perfect, Past Perfect Continuous.
* **Future Expressions**: *Will* vs. *Going to*, Present Continuous for future, Future Perfect, Future Continuous.

**3. Sentence Structure & Syntax**

* **Clause Types**: Independent, dependent, relative clauses (defining vs. non-defining).
* **Word Order**: Standard SVOMPT (Subject - Verb - Object - Manner - Place - Time), inversion for emphasis.
* **Question Formation**: Open/closed questions, subject/object questions, tag questions, indirect questions.

**4. Advanced & Functional Grammar**

* **Conditionals & Hypotheses**: Zero, 1st, 2nd, 3rd, mixed conditionals, *wish* / *if only*.
* **Voice & Style**: Active vs. passive voice, reported speech (backshifting tenses), causative forms (*have something done*).
* **Gerunds & Infinitives**: Verbs followed by *-ing*, verbs followed by *to + base*, bare infinitives.

---
For a purely factual reference site, the information should follow a **hierarchical, taxonomy-based structure** (from smallest functional units to complex sentence systems) rather than a pedagogical learning path.
## Master Reference Taxonomy

**1. Lexical Grammar (Word Classes & Morphology)**

* **Nouns & Nominal Forms**: Proper/Common, Countable/Uncountable, Pluralization rules, Possessives.
* **Determiners**: Articles (*a, an, the*), Demonstratives (*this, that*), Quantifiers, Possessive Determiners.
* **Pronouns**: Personal, Reflexive, Relative, Interrogative, Indefinite, Demonstrative.
* **Verbs (Lexical)**: Transitive/Intransitive, State/Dynamic, Regular/Irregular forms, Phrasal verbs.
* **Adjectives**: Attributive/Predicative position, Order of Adjectives, Comparative/Superlative morphology.
* **Adverbs**: Classification (Time, Place, Manner, Degree, Frequency), Position rules.
* **Prepositions**: Simple, Compound, Dependent prepositions (Verb/Adjective complementation).
* **Conjunctions**: Coordinating (*FANBOYS*), Subordinating, Correlative (*either…or*).

**2. Verb Systems (Tense, Aspect, Voice & Mood)**

* **Tense & Aspect Matrix**: Simple, Continuous, Perfect, and Perfect Continuous across Present, Past, and Future.
* **Auxiliaries & Modals**: Primary Auxiliaries (*be, do, have*), Core Modals (*can, must, should*), Semi-Modals (*ought to, used to*).
* **Voice**: Active Voice, Passive Voice, Agentless Passive, Causatives (*have/get something done*).
* **Mood & Modality**: Indicative, Imperative, Subjunctive (*if I were*, *it is crucial that he be*).

**3. Syntax & Clause Structure**

* **Basic Sentence Mechanics**: Word order patterns (SVO, SVOC, SVOA), Subject-Verb Agreement rules.
* **Clause Types**: Independent Clauses, Subordinate Clauses (Noun, Adjective/Relative, Adverbial).
* **Non-Finite Structures**: Infinitive phrases, Gerund phrases, Participle clauses.
* **Sentence Transformations**: Direct/Indirect Questions, Inversion, Cleft sentences (*It was X that…*).

**4. Punctuation & Orthography**

* **Sentence Boundaries**: Periods, Question Marks, Exclamation Points.
* **Internal Clause Mechanics**: Commas, Semicolons, Colons, Dashes, Hyphens.
* **Typography & Mechanics**: Capitalization rules, Apostrophes (possession vs. contraction), Quotation Marks.

## Reference Page Blueprint

To maintain a strict reference tone across every page, use a standardized, factual schema:

| Section | Factual Content |
| --- | --- |
| **Definition** | Standard linguistic classification and structural role. |
| **Morphological Rules** | Formulas, inflection suffixes, or regular/irregular paradigms. |
| **Syntactic Position** | Exact positioning constraints within a clause. |
| **Distribution & Usage** | Enumerated rules with illustrative example sentences. |
| **Exceptions & Edge Cases** | Irregularities, non-standard variants, or regional differences (UK vs. US). |

**Reiwa's ESL Quick Reference Handbook (QRH)** is a free, concise and comprehensive digital reference guide designed for learners of English as a Second Language. It provides quick access to core English language rules, structural explanations and supporting learning resources without lengthy lesson modules.

Built by professional language instructors for independent learners.
