## Types of Adverbs
To learn more, such as adverbs often used in natural English conversation, as well as understanding their use in context, please read the pages below.

### Adverbs of Time

> **Adverbs of Time** describe *when* an action takes place.
> They can be used to add context to actions that occur at specific times.

[[time|Adverbs of Time]]

### Adverbs of Frequency

> **Adverbs of Frequency** describe *how often* an action occurs or is performed.
> They can be used to add context to actions that happen *more than once*.

[[frequency|Adverbs of Frequency]]

### Adverbs of Place

> **Adverbs of Place** describe where an action takes place.
> They can be used to add context to actions that happen at specific locations.

[[place|Adverbs of Place]]

### Adverbs of Manner

> **Adverbs of Manner** describe how an action occurs or is performed.
> They can be used to add context to actions that are performed in specific ways.

[[manner|Adverbs of Manner]]

### Adverbs of Degree

> **Adverbs of Degree** describe how much an action occurs or is performed.
> They can be used to add context to actions that are performed in specific amounts.

[[degree|Adverbs of Degree]]

### Adverbs of Certainty
> **Adverbs of Certainty** describe how *sure or confident* we are about an action or event.
> They can be used to express levels of probability ranging from slight doubt to complete certainty.

[[certainty|Adverbs of Certainty]]

### Conjunctive Adverbs
> **Conjunctive Adverbs** connect two independent clauses or sentences together.
> They are used to indicate relationships such as cause and effect, contrast, sequence or addition between ideas.

[[conjunctive|Conjunctive Adverbs]]

### Interrogative Adverbs
> **Interrogative Adverbs** are used at the beginning of a sentence to ask a question.
> They allow us to inquire about time, place, reason or manner (when, where, why, how).

[[interrogative|Interrogative Adverbs]]
